What is the system?
 An Electric Overhead Traveling (EOT) Crane, consisting of a translating bridge, a traversing trolley, and a hoisting cable with a heavy payload.

Why does it matter? 
In industrial warehouses, moving heavy payloads quickly causes dangerous pendulum swing. Understanding the dynamics is critical for designing anti-sway motor controllers.

Problem Statement:
How do the accelerations of the bridge and the hoisting speed of the cable couple together to induce or amplify payload swing?

Degrees of Freedom:
 5 DOF (Bridge X, Trolley Y, Hoist Z, Swing θx, Swing θy) —
Key Assumptions :
Rigid Body Dynamics: The steel bridge girders do not deform at operating speeds.
Particle Kinetics: Cable length is much larger than the payload, allowing it to be treated as a point mass.
Small Angle Approximation: Swing angles rarely exceed 15 degrees; therefore, sin(θ) ≈ θ.

4. Mathematical Derivation (Part 1)
Method: Newton's Second Law of Motion 

Why this method? 
Newton's laws were chosen over Lagrange's equations because they explicitly expose the internal constraint force (cable tension, T). Knowing real-time tension is critical for industrial cable sizing and safety factors.

Step 1 (Kinematics): Define absolute payload acceleration using the product rule:

Apx = ẍ + (L̈ * θx) + (2 * L̇ * θ̇x) + (L * θ̈x)

Step 2 (Z-Axis Kinetics): Sum forces vertically for payload mass (mp):

 mp * Apz = mp * g - T —> T = mp * (g - L̈)

Step 3 (X-Axis Kinetics): Sum forces horizontally for payload mass: 

mp * Apx = -T * θx
5. Final Equations & State-Space
Final Equations of Motion:

EOM 1 (Payload Swing): 

L * θ̈x + 2 * L̇ * θ̇x + g * θx = -ẍ

EOM 2 (Bridge Translation): 

Mb * ẍ = Fx - cx * ẋ + mp * (g - L̈) * θx

8. Validation & Analysis
Validation 1 (Energy Check): 
Total energy perfectly conserved (red line flat), proving the derived equations respect physical laws.

Validation 2 (Analytical Comparison): 
The simulated time period for a 10m cable was 6.34 seconds. This perfectly matches the textbook natural frequency calculation: ω_n = √(g/L) = √(9.81/10) = 0.99 rad/s.

Sensitivity Analysis:
Varied parameter: Hoist Cable Length (L).
Effect: Shortening the cable to 5m increased the oscillation frequency. Lengthening it to 15m slowed the swing. Hoisting upwards makes the system more highly oscillatory.

Limitations: 
The small angle assumption (sin(θ) ≈ θ) will fail and underestimate restoring forces if sudden braking causes a swing greater than 15-20 degrees.
9. Conclusions & Reflections
Key Finding: 
Pendulum swing in cranes isn't just an isolated pendulum; it is actively driven by bridge acceleration and modified by Coriolis forces during hoisting.

What we learned (Beyond the Textbook): 
Textbooks teach static natural frequencies. This project taught us how active length changes (L̇) dynamically shift the system's behavior in real-time.
