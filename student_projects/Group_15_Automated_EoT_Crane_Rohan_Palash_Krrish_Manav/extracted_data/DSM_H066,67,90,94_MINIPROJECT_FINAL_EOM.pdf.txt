Phase 2: Mathematical Model
Method Chosen: Newton's Second Law of Motion (Newton-Euler
Formulation)
Justification: While Lagrange's equations are excellent for energy-based
systems, Newton's Laws are chosen here because they explicitly expose the
internal reaction forces (specifically, the cable tension). In industrial crane design,
knowing the real-time tension is critical for cable sizing and safety factors.
Furthermore, breaking the system down into individual free-body diagrams
makes the physical coupling between the motors and the payload swing highly
intuitive.
1. Coordinate System & Small Angle Assumption
 We define the Z-axis as positive downwards (direction of gravity).
 To make the step-by-step derivation manageable and realistic for control
design, we apply the Small Angle Approximation for the payload swing.
In industrial cranes, swing angles rarely exceed 10-15 degrees.
 Therefore: sin(θ) is approximately equal to θ, and cos(θ) is approximately
equal to 1.
2. Kinematic Equations (Position, Velocity, Acceleration)
First, we define the absolute position of the payload mass (mp) in 3D space. It
depends on the bridge position (x), trolley position (y), cable length (L), and the
small swing angles (θx and θy).
 Payload Position (Xp, Yp, Zp):
i. Xp = x + L * θx
ii. Yp = y + L * θy
iii. Zp = L
Next, we take the first derivative with respect to time to get the velocities. We
must use the product rule since the cable length (L) can change while hoisting.
 Payload Velocity (Vpx, Vpy, Vpz):
i. Vpx = ẋ + (L̇ * θx) + (L * θ̇x)
ii. Vpy = ẏ + (L̇ * θy) + (L * θ̇y)
iii. Vpz = L̇
Taking the second derivative gives the absolute accelerations.
 Payload Acceleration (Apx, Apy, Apz):
i. Apx = ẍ + (L̈ * θx) + (2 * L̇ * θẋ ) + (L * θ̈x)
ii. Apy = ÿ + (L̈ * θy) + (2 * L̇ * θẏ ) + (L * θ̈y)
iii. Apz = L̈
3. Applying Newton's Second Law (Sum of Forces = Mass * Acceleration)
We now analyze the Free Body Diagram for each mass component.
Step A: The Payload (mp) in the Z-axis (Vertical)
Forces acting on the payload in the Z direction: Gravity pulling down (+mp * g)
and Cable Tension pulling up (-T).
Equation: mp * Apz = mp * g - T
Substitute Apz: mp * L̈ = mp * g - T
Rearranging for Tension (T): T = mp * (g - L̈ )
Step B: The Payload (mp) in the X-axis and Y-axis (Swing)
Forces acting on the payload in the horizontal directions come entirely from the
horizontal components of the cable tension. For small angles, the horizontal
tension is T * θ. Because tension pulls the payload back towards the vertical
center, it is negative.
X-Axis Equation: mp * Apx = -T * θx
Y-Axis Equation: mp * Apy = -T * θy
Step 3C: Deriving the Swing Equations of Motion
Let's substitute our Apx and T equations into the X-Axis Payload equation.
 mp * [ẍ + (L̈ * θx) + (2 * L̇ * θẋ ) + (L * θ̈x)] = - [mp * (g - L̈ )] * θx
Divide both sides by mass (mp) to simplify:
 ẍ + (L̈ * θx) + (2 * L̇ * θẋ ) + (L * θẍ ) = -g * θx + (L̈ * θx)
Notice that the term (L̈ * θx) exists on both sides and cancels out. Rearranging
the remaining terms gives us our final payload swing equation for the X-axis. The
Y-axis follows the exact same symmetrical logic.
4. Final Equations of Motion (EOM)
EOM 1: Payload Swing in X-Z Plane
 L * θẍ + 2 * L̇ * θẋ + g * θx = -ẍ
(Physical Meaning: This reveals that the bridge acceleration, ẍ, acts as a driving
force that causes the pendulum to swing. The term 2 * L̇ * θ̇x is the Coriolis
acceleration that occurs when the payload is hoisted while swinging.)
EOM 2: Payload Swing in Y-Z Plane
 L * θ̈y + 2 * L̇ * θẏ + g * θy = -ÿ
(Physical Meaning: Mirrors the X-axis; the trolley acceleration acts as the
external disturbance causing the payload to oscillate in the Y direction.)
EOM 3: Bridge Dynamics (Mass = Mb)
Forces: Motor Force (Fx), Friction opposing motion (-cx * ẋ), and the horizontal
pull of the cable tension from the swinging payload (+T * θx).
 Mb * ẍ = Fx - cx * ẋ + mp * (g - L̈ ) * θx
(Physical Meaning: To move the bridge, the motor must overcome the bridge
inertia, track friction, and the reactive tugging force of the swinging payload.)
EOM 4: Trolley Dynamics (Mass = mt)
Forces: Motor Force (Fy), Friction (-cy * ẏ), and cable tension horizontal pull
(+T * θy).
 mt * ÿ = Fy - cy * ẏ + mp * (g - L̈ ) * θy
(Physical Meaning: The trolley motor must overcome trolley inertia, friction, and
the Y-axis pendulum reaction force.)
EOM 5: Hoist Dynamics
If we assume the hoist motor directly controls the vertical force (Fz) acting on the
cable:
 mp * L̈ = mp * g - Fz
(Physical Meaning: If the hoist motor force equals the weight of the payload,
acceleration is zero and it hoists at a constant velocity. If it drops below payload
weight, the load accelerates downward.)
5. Units Consistency Check
Let us verify EOM 1: L * θẍ + 2 * L̇ * θ̇x + g * θx = -ẍ
 L * θẍ : (meters) * (1 / seconds²) = m/s²
 2 * L̇ * θẋ : (meters / second) * (1 / second) = m/s²
 g * θx: (m/s²) * (dimensionless radians) = m/s²
 -ẍ: m/s²
All terms share the unit of linear acceleration (m/s²), confirming dimensional
consistency across the derived dynamic model.
