1. Introduction
Hexapod robots are bio-inspired locomotion systems that replicate the walking mechanisms
of insects and crustaceans. These robots are capable of stable locomotion because their six-
leg configuration allows multiple legs to maintain contact with the ground at all times.
Each leg of a hexapod robot typically consists of three rotational joints: the coxa, femur, and
tibia. These joints allow the robot to move its legs in different directions and generate the
walking motion required for locomotion.
To analyze the motion and dynamics of the robot, it is necessary to develop a mathematical
model describing the relationship between joint torques, forces, and resulting motion. In this
work, the equations of motion (EOM) for a hexapod leg are derived using the Lagrangian
method, which is widely used in robotics and dynamic system modeling.
The derived equations will later be useful for simulation, gait planning, stability analysis, and
control system design.

3. Modeling Assumptions
To simplify the modeling process, the following assumptions are made:
1. The robot body is treated as a rigid body.
2. Each leg is modeled as a three-link planar manipulator.
3. The ground surface is assumed to be flat and rigid.
4. The foot does not slip during ground contact.
5. Air resistance is neglected.
6. Friction at the joints is negligible.
7. The center of mass of each link lies at the geometric center of the link.
These assumptions reduce the complexity of the dynamic equations.

5. Choice of Dynamic Modeling Method
The Lagrangian formulation is used to derive the equations of motion.
The Lagrangian method is preferred because:
• It uses generalized coordinates, which simplifies modeling of multi-link robotic
systems.
• It avoids calculating forces individually for each link.
• It provides a systematic approach to deriving dynamic equations.
Therefore, the Lagrangian method is well suited for multi-joint robotic systems such as
hexapod legs.

17. Conclusion
A dynamic model of a hexapod robot leg with three joints (coxa, femur, tibia) was developed.
The kinematic equations describing foot position were derived, followed by velocity and
acceleration expressions. Using the Lagrangian formulation, the equations of motion were
obtained.
The final equations describe the relationship between joint torques and leg motion, providing
a foundation for simulation, gait design, and control of the hexapod robot.
