System Identification Report
Project Title: Quadruped Leg
Phase: Phase 1 – System Identification
Date: 25/04/26

1. System Selection & Approval
The system selected is a single leg of a quadruped robot, modeled as a planar multi-link mechanism. This system was chosen from the provided problem bank and approved by the instructor.

2. Real-World Application
Quadruped leg mechanisms are widely used in robotics for locomotion, such as robotic dogs and terrain exploration robots, where stable and adaptive walking is required.

3. System Description
The quadruped leg is modeled as a 2-link or 3-link articulated system connected by rotary joints (hip, knee, and optionally ankle). The motion is constrained to a 2D plane for simplicity.

4. Inputs, Outputs, State Variables, Parameters
Inputs:
Joint torques (τ₁, τ₂) or joint angles (θ₁, θ₂)
Outputs:
Position of foot (end-effector): (x, y)
State Variables:
Joint angles: θ₁, θ₂
Angular velocities: θ̇₁, θ̇₂
Parameters:
Link lengths: L₁, L₂
Mass of links: m₁, m₂
Gravitational acceleration: g

5. Degrees of Freedom (DOF)
The system has 2 degrees of freedom, corresponding to the two independent joint rotations (hip and knee).
Each joint contributes one rotational DOF, and the motion of the leg is fully described by these two variables.

6. Key Assumptions (with reasoning)
Planar motion: Motion is restricted to 2D to simplify analysis while still capturing essential behavior.
Rigid links: Links are assumed rigid to avoid deformation complexity.
Frictionless joints: Neglecting friction simplifies dynamic equations and focuses on primary motion.
Point masses at link centers: Simplifies inertia calculations while maintaining physical relevance.
Uniform gravity: Assumed constant for all calculations.

8. Coordinate System & Sign Convention
Origin is fixed at the hip joint
X-axis: horizontal direction
Y-axis: vertical upward direction
Joint angles (θ₁, θ₂) are measured counterclockwise from the horizontal
Positive rotation: counterclockwise

Conclusion
The system has been clearly identified with defined inputs, outputs, assumptions, and physical structure. This forms the foundation for further kinematic and dynamic modeling in later phases.
