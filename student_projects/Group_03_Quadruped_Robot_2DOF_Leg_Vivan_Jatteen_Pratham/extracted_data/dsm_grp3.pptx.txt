System Introduction & Motivation
What is the System?
A quadruped robot (inspired by Unitree Go1) with 2 degrees of freedom per leg — hip joint angle θi,1 and knee joint angle θi,2 — operating in the sagittal plane.
Engineering Relevance
Legged locomotion over unstructured terrain
Search-and-rescue and field robotics
Adaptive gait planning and stability control

PBL Question: How do we mathematically model the dynamic behaviour of a 2-DOF robotic leg under ground reaction forces?
Problem Formulation & Assumptions
System Variables

Inputs
Joint torques τhip, τknee

Outputs
θi,1(t) and θi,2(t) — hip & knee angles

States
θi,1, θ̇i,1, θi,2, θ̇i,2

Parameters
m, L1, L2, I1, I2, c, g
Modelling Assumptions
Rigid links — no structural deformation
Planar (sagittal) motion only — 2-DOF justified
Viscous damping at each joint
No-slip foot contact with ground
Point mass approximation for link inertia

DOF = 2 per leg, confirmed by restricting motion to the sagittal plane — a standard simplification for gait analysis.

Free Body Diagram — 2-DOF Leg

Hip Joint (θi,1)
Torque τhip applied. Upper link L1, mass m1. Gravity acts at link centroid.

Knee Joint (θi,2)
Torque τknee applied. Lower link L2, mass m2. Coupled via hip kinematics.

Ground Reaction Ni
Vertical reaction force at foot contact point. Acts upward, opposing gravity-induced sag.

Coordinate Frame
Origin at hip joint. Angles measured from vertical. Clockwise positive convention.

Known Limitations
Rigid body assumption — no link flexibility
No terrain interaction or foot-slip modelled
Cross-coupling between hip and knee omitted in simplified EOM
2D sagittal plane only — no lateral dynamics
Conclusions, Reflections & References
Key Findings
The 2-DOF Lagrangian model accurately captures joint dynamics with energy decay under damping
Most challenging: CO4 — deriving and interpreting coupled EOMs
Future improvement: include hip-knee cross-coupling terms for full accuracy
