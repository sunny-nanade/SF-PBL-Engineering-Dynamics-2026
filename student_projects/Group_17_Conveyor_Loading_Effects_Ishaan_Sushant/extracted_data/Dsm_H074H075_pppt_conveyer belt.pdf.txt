01
System Introduction & Motivation
What is the system? A motorized conveyor belt
transporting a block load governed by Coulomb
friction — belt moves at constant 1.5 m/s, block
placed on it from rest
Why does it matter? Variable loads cause speed
mismatches, friction-driven acceleration, and
positional drift — critical in manufacturing, mining,
and baggage handling systems
PBL Problem Statement: How does a block's
velocity and position respond when placed on a
moving conveyor belt with dry Coulomb friction
under variable load conditions?
02
Problem Formulation &
Assumptions
Inputs: Belt velocity V_belt = 1.5 m/s (constant), block mass m = 1.0
kg
Outputs: Block velocity v(t) [m/s], Block position x(t) [m]
State Variables: v(t), x(t)
Parameters: m = 1.0 kg, μ = 0.35, g = 9.81 m/s², V_belt = 1.5 m/s
DOF: 1 — translational motion along belt only; horizontal plane, no
vertical dynamics
Assumptions:Point mass model — no rotation
Constant belt velocity — motor dynamics excluded
Coulomb friction only — μ constant, dry, no air resistance, rigid
surface

05
Final Equations & State-Space
Final EOM:
dv/dt = μ·g·sign(V_belt − v) dx/dt = v
State-Space Form:
State vector: X = [v, x]ᵀ
ẋ₁ = μ·g·sign(1.5 − x₁)
ẋ₂ = x₁
(Non-linear due to sign function — no standard A,B,C,D matrices)
Physical meaning of each term (CO3):
μ·g = maximum friction acceleration = 0.35 × 9.81 = 3.434 m/s²
sign(V_belt − v) = direction of friction: +1 when block slower than belt, 0 when
equal
Energy expression (CO5):
KE(t) = ½·m·v(t)²
Work done by friction = μ·m·g·Δx (until v = V_belt)

Sensitivity Analysis: Varying μ from 0.2→0.5 → acceleration time changes

09
. Conclusions & Reflections
Key Finding: A block placed on a moving conveyor belt accelerates linearly
under Coulomb friction (dv/dt = μg = 3.43 m/s²) and locks to belt speed (1.5
m/s) in 0.43 s — matching analytical prediction exactly
What simulation taught beyond textbooks: Observing the phase portrait as a
flat horizontal line made it visually clear that Coulomb friction produces
constant acceleration unlike viscous damping — this cannot be appreciated
from equations alone
What we'd do differently: Include belt elasticity (spring model) and add
variable load events to observe transient recovery
Most critical course units: Newton's 2nd Law (CO4), Force identification
(CO3), Energy methods (CO5)
