1. System Introduction & Motivation
What is the system?
A shock absorber is a mechanical device used in vehicle suspension systems to dissipate vibrational energy.
Why does it matter?
Improves ride comfort
Ensures tire-road contact
Reduces structural damage

How does a shock absorber dissipate energy over time due to damping?

2. Problem Formulation & Assumptions
Inputs: External force F(t)

Outputs: Displacement x(t)

State Variables: x, x˙

Parameters:
        Mass m
        Damping coefficient c
        Spring stiffness k

DOF: 1 DOF (vertical motion only)

Assumptions:
       Linear spring → F=kx
       Viscous damping → Fd=cx
       No friction except damping
       Small oscillations (linear system)

9. Conclusions & Reflections
Key Finding: 
The shock absorber behaves as a mass–spring–damper system with damped oscillations.
Energy dissipates over time due to viscous damping (cx’), converting mechanical energy into heat.
The system shows exponential decay in amplitude, confirming realistic physical behavior.
Simulation results match theoretical expectations, validating the mathematical model.

What we learned that a textbook couldn't teach:
The importance of correct sign convention in FBD and equations—small mistakes change results completely.
How to connect mathematical equations with physical meaning (especially damping and energy loss).
Practical understanding of energy decay and power dissipation, not just formulas.
Difficulty in translating real-world systems into simplified models with justified assumptions.
CO4 & CO5 were most challenging due to derivation and energy interpretation.

What we would do differently (Point-wise)
Start with a clear and verified Free Body Diagram earlier.
Fix sign conventions before derivation to avoid rework.
Perform parameter sensitivity analysis earlier in the process.
