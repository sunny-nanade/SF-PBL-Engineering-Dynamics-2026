1. ABSTRACT
System identification involves developing mathematical models of dynamic systems using
observed or assumed data. In this project, a shock absorber is modelled as a mass-spring-damper
system to study energy loss characteristics. The damping effect is analyzed to understand how
mechanical energy is dissipated over time. Simulation is performed to validate the system
behavior.
2. INTRODUCTION
Shock absorbers are essential components in mechanical and automotive systems. They reduce
vibrations and improve system stability by dissipating energy.
System identification helps in representing such real-world systems mathematically and
predicting their behavior under different conditions.
3. PROBLEM STATEMENT
To develop a mathematical model of a shock absorber and analyze how energy is lost due to
damping.
4. OBJECTIVES
• To model the shock absorber as a dynamic system
• To identify system parameters
• To analyze energy dissipation
• To simulate system behavior
5. LITERATURE REVIEW
Previous studies show that shock absorbers can be modeled using second-order differential
equations. Numerical methods like Euler and Runge-Kutta are used for simulation. Damping
plays a key role in controlling oscillations and energy loss.
6. REAL WORLD SYSTEM
In real-world applications, shock absorbers are used in vehicles to reduce vibrations caused by
road irregularities.
When a vehicle encounters a bump:
• The spring stores energy
• The damper dissipates energy as heat
• Oscillations gradually decrease
7. SYSTEM DESCRIPTION
The shock absorber is modelled as a mass-spring-damper system:
• Mass (m): Vehicle body
• Spring (k): Stores energy
• Damper (c): Dissipates energy
Input: Road disturbance
Output: Displacement

15. RESULTS
The simulation of the mass-spring-damper model of the shock absorber provides important
insights into the system behavior.
Initially, when the system is disturbed (given an initial displacement), the mass begins to oscillate
due to the restoring force of the spring. However, unlike an ideal system, these oscillations do not
continue indefinitely. The presence of the damper introduces a resistive force proportional to
velocity, which gradually reduces the amplitude of oscillations.
The displacement vs time graph shows a decaying oscillatory response, indicating that the
system is underdamped. The peaks of the oscillations decrease exponentially with time,
demonstrating the effect of energy dissipation.
The energy plot clearly shows that the total mechanical energy of the system decreases
continuously. At the beginning, the system has maximum energy due to initial displacement. As
time progresses:
• Kinetic energy increases and decreases periodically
• Potential energy is exchanged with kinetic energy
• A portion of energy is continuously lost due to damping
This energy loss follows the relation:
• Energy decay is proportional to the square of velocity
• Higher velocity → higher energy loss
Eventually, the system reaches equilibrium where:
• Displacement → 0
• Velocity → 0
• Total energy → minimum (almost zero)
This confirms that the shock absorber effectively removes unwanted vibrations.
16. DISCUSSION
The results obtained from the simulation strongly support the theoretical model of the shock
absorber as a mass-spring-damper system.
One of the most important observations is the role of the damping coefficient (c). The value of
damping determines how quickly the system loses energy and stabilizes:
• If damping is low → oscillations persist for a longer time
• If damping is high → oscillations decay quickly
• If damping is too high → system becomes overdamped and responds slowly
Thus, an optimal value of damping is required in practical systems to balance comfort and
stability.
Another key observation is that energy loss is not constant. It depends on velocity:
• At higher velocities → more energy is dissipated
• At lower velocities → energy loss decreases
This matches real-world behavior of hydraulic shock absorbers where fluid resistance increases
with speed.
The model used in this project assumes:
• Linear damping
• No external disturbances
• Ideal spring behavior
However, real systems may exhibit:
• Nonlinear damping
• Temperature effects
• Variable fluid viscosity
Despite these simplifications, the model provides a very good approximation of real shock
absorber behavior and is widely used in engineering analysis.
17. CONCLUSION
In this project, a shock absorber system was successfully modeled using system identification
techniques. The system was represented as a mass-spring-damper model, and its behavior was
analyzed through mathematical derivation and simulation.
The study clearly demonstrates that damping plays a crucial role in reducing oscillations and
stabilizing the system. The derived energy equation shows that the rate of energy loss is
proportional to the square of velocity, confirming that energy is continuously dissipated in the
form of heat.
Simulation results validate the theoretical model by showing:
• Decaying oscillations over time
• Continuous reduction in system energy
• Stabilization of the system at equilibrium
This confirms that shock absorbers are effective in converting unwanted mechanical energy into
thermal energy, thereby improving system performance and comfort.
Overall, the system identification approach proves to be a powerful tool for modeling and
analyzing real-world dynamic systems. The findings of this project can be applied to various
engineering applications such as vehicle suspension systems, vibration control, and structural
stability.
