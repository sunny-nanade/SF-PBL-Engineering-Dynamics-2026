Modelling energy loss in Shock Absorber

Introduction
Shock absorbers are critical components in vehicle suspension systems, designed to dissipate
kinetic energy and reduce the impact of road irregularities. Proper modeling of a shock
absorber allows engineers to analyze its damping characteristics, stiffness, and response to
transient loads.
This work derives the equations of motion (EOM) for a simplified shock absorber model
using the Lagrangian method. The resulting model is essential for simulation, ride comfort
analysis, stability assessment, and suspension control system design.
System Description
The shock absorber is modelled as a one-degree-of-freedom (1-DOF) spring-damper-mass
system.
Components:
• Mass (m): Represents the unsprung mass (wheel, axle, etc.).
• Spring (k): Represents the stiffness of the shock absorber.
• Damper (c): Represents the damping coefficient (viscous friction).
• Base: Connected to the vehicle chassis (moving or fixed).
The system is assumed to operate vertically.
Modelling Assumptions
1. The mass moves only in the vertical direction (1-DOF).
2. The spring is linear (force proportional to displacement).
3. The damper is linear viscous (force proportional to velocity).
4. The shock absorber housing is rigid.
5. Air resistance is neglected.
6. No internal friction other than the damper.
7. The base excitation (from road) is known or assumed sinusoidal.
8. These assumptions yield a linear second-order ODE.

Choice of Dynamic Modeling Method
The Lagrangian method is used. It is suitable because:
• It handles potential and kinetic energy systematically.
• It naturally includes dissipative forces via Rayleigh dissipation function.
• It provides a clean path to equations of motion without free-body diagrams.
