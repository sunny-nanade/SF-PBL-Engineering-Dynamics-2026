Q1: What was the single hardest technical challenge you faced in deriving the mathematical
model? How did you resolve it?
• The main challenge was accurately modeling the damping and spring stiffness
interaction to capture energy dissipation over compression and extension cycles.
• The difficulty came from coupling the nonlinear damping term (proportional to
velocity) with varying spring compression rates.
• We resolved this by analyzing simulation outputs from PyBullet that tracked spring
force, displacement, and velocity over time, and then applying numerical integration to
compute energy loss per cycle using the area under the force–displacement curve.
Q2: Which Course Outcome (CO1–CO5) was most challenging for your team? Why?
• CO4 – Implementing and interpreting numerical simulations for dynamic systems was
the hardest.
• This is because integrating the physical model with PyBullet required understanding
URDF dynamics, correctly defining prismatic joint behavior, and controlling the
alternating forces (+1500 N, -1250 N) to simulate realistic oscillation.
• Achieving stable simulation cycles without divergence forced the team to fine-tune
simulation parameters like time step and pause duration.
Q3: What would you do differently if you started the project over?
• Start by building the simplified analytical model first, then validate it with simulation
instead of the other way around.
• Include experimental damping coefficients earlier to better match realistic energy losses.
• Automate data extraction from simulation logs for faster plotting and energy
computations across multiple cycles.
Q4: What did you learn from this project that a regular textbook problem could NOT teach
you?
• How dynamic systems behave under non-ideal, time-dependent forces — something
static textbook problems ignore.
• The visualization of spring compression and extension phases (red/green/yellow in the
simulation) gave insight into transient response, phase lag, and damping behavior.
• We also learned to handle numerical instabilities and real-time simulation control in a
physics engine.
Q5: How did you divide work among team members? Did everyone understand the full
derivation?
• One member handled URDF and PyBullet setup, another conducted data collection and
plotting, and the third derived the mathematical energy loss model (E = ∫F·dx).
• All members discussed model validation and reviewed derived equations to ensure
everyone understood how force, velocity, and displacement contributed to energy
dissipation.
• Team review sessions helped ensure shared understanding before compiling final
results.
Q6: What AI tools did you use? For what specific purpose? What did you NOT use AI for?
• Used AI-assisted coding (ChatGPT) to debug Python-PyBullet force control loops and
improve plotting scripts.
• Used AI for documentation summarization and code comments generation.
• Did NOT use AI for deriving the physical equations or performing numerical
integration — those were handled by the team to maintain conceptual rigor and learning
integrity.
Summary Insight:
The simulation demonstrated that energy loss per compression-extension cycle increases with
higher applied force amplitude and shorter pause durations, effectively showing how damping
stabilizes oscillations in a real shock absorber.
