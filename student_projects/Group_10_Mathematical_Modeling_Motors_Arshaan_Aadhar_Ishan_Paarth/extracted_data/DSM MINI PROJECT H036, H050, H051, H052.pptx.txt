1. System Introduction & Motivation
The system under study consists of three types of electric motors 
	DC, stepper and servo motor, which convert electrical energy into mechanical motion.
A DC motor provides continuous rotational motion.
A stepper motor produces discrete angular steps.
A servo motor enables precise position, velocity, and acceleration control using feedback.
These motors are fundamental in modern engineering systems
DC Motors → conveyors, fans, electric vehicles
Stepper Motors → 3D printers, CNC machines, robotics
Servo Motors → robotic arms, aerospace systems, automation

What question are we answering?
	How can we mathematically model DC, stepper, and servo motors using physical laws 	(FBD, 	electrical laws), and represent them in forms suitable for analysis such as 	differential equations and state-space models?

 Problem Formulation & Assumptions for DC Motor
Inputs
Voltage: V(t)
Load torque disturbance: T_L(t)

Outputs
Angular velocity: \omega(t) and Angular position: \theta(t)

State Variables
x_1 = \omega(t) (angular velocity)
x_2 = i(t) (armature current)

Parameters
J: Moment of inertia
B: Viscous friction coefficient
R: Armature resistance
L: Armature inductance
K_t: Torque constant
K_e: Back EMF constant

DOF = 1. Only one independent mechanical motion: shaft rotation

 Problem Formulation & Assumptions for Stepper Motor
Inputs
Phase voltages: V_A(t), V_B(t)
Pulse sequence (digital control input)

Outputs
Angular position: \theta(t)
Step position (discrete)

State Variables
x_1 = \theta(t)
x_2 = \dot{\theta}(t)
x_3 = i_A(t)
x_4 = i_B(t)

Parameters
J: Rotor inertia
B: Damping coefficient
R, L: Phase resistance & inductance
K_m: Motor torque constant

DOF = 1. Only one mechanical motion: rotor angle (theta)

 Problem Formulation & Assumptions for Servo Motor
Inputs
Control voltage: V(t)
Reference input (desired position): \theta_{ref}(t)
Load torque: T_L(t)

Outputs
Angular position: \theta(t)
Angular velocity: \dot{\theta}(t)

State Variables
x_1 = \theta(t)
x_2 = \dot{\theta}(t)
x_3 = i(t)

Parameters
J: Inertia
B: Damping coefficient
R, L: Electrical parameters
K_t, K_e: Motor constants
Controller gain (if included): K_p, K_d, etc.

DOF = 1. Only one motion: shaft rotation.

9. Conclusions & Reflections
The mathematical models of DC, stepper, and servo motors were successfully derived and validated, showing that DC and servo motors are linear systems while the stepper motor is nonlinear.

Key Finding: The models accurately represent electromechanical energy conversion and system dynamics, matching standard textbook formulations.

What we learned that a textbook couldn’t teach: Converting real systems into equations requires proper assumptions and interpretation; CO2 (FBD) was the most challenging concept.

What we would do differently: Start early with simulation tools and improve validation of assumptions and intermediate steps.
