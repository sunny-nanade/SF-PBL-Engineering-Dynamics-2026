System Identification Report
Topic: Dynamic Modelling of a 12-DOF Biped Robot Using Euler-Lagrange Mechanics in PyBullet
By: H003,H006,H024,H029
1. System Selection and Approval
The selected system is a lower-body biped robot model implemented in PyBullet and described by a URDF file.
It is treated as a nonlinear multi-body dynamic system suitable for dynamic systems analysis, modelling,
simulation, and validation through joint-space and contact-force responses.
2. Real-World Application
The system represents a humanoid lower-body platform relevant to legged locomotion, balance control,
rehabilitation robotics, humanoid platforms, and dynamic gait research. Such systems must coordinate contact
forces, joint torques, link inertia, and center-of-mass motion during tasks such as walking, squatting, and
jumping.
3. System Definition
3.1 Inputs
• Joint-space actuation commands applied through PyBullet position control.
• Task-space leg position references used by the inverse-kinematics routine.
• Gait-level inputs such as stride, step height, and support-point progression during walking.
• Terrain inclination through the plane orientation parameter.
3.2 Outputs
• Joint angles, joint torques, and grouped joint stress proxies.
• Ground reaction force magnitude and components.
• Kinetic, potential, and total energy of grouped hip, knee, and ankle subsystems.
• Base pose, link pose, foot trajectories, and center-of-mass trajectory.

3.4 Key Parameters
• Total robot mass = 5.955684 kg
• Body mass = 4.546 kg
• Right leg mass = 0.704842 kg
• Leg-link lengths used in kinematics: L1 = 0.18 m, L2 = 0.18 m
• Gravity = 9.81 m/s²
• Per-joint limit range = [-2.5, 2.5] rad, effort limit = 8 N·m, velocity limit = 6 rad/s
• Nominal simulation step = 1/240 s
4. Degrees of Freedom (DOF)
DOF = 12 actuated lower-body degrees of freedom.
• Right leg: hip yaw, hip roll, hip pitch, knee pitch, ankle pitch, ankle roll
• Left leg: hip yaw, hip roll, hip pitch, knee pitch, ankle pitch, ankle roll
This gives full sagittal and lateral lower-body actuation required for standing, walking, squatting, and jumping
tasks.

6. Key Assumptions
• All links are rigid bodies with constant mass and inertia.
• Joint friction and motor limits are taken from the URDF and PyBullet setup.
• Ground interaction is concentrated through contact points at the foot links.
• The lower-body model is analysed as an actuated multi-body system in a controlled environment.
• Aerodynamic and structural compliance effects are neglected.
• The analytical model is Euler-Lagrange based; PyBullet supplies the numerical rigid-body simulation.
