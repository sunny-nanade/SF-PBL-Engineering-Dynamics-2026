1. System Introduction & Motivation
The project studies the lower-body dynamics of a simulated humanoid biped and focuses on analysis-ready outputs rather than animation alone.






System
A PyBullet humanoid lower body is modeled from URDF link masses, inertias, and joint limits. Each leg carries six actuated joints, while the analysis groups behavior into Hip, Knee, and Ankle levels.






Motivation
Dynamic systems analysis needs measurable signals such as GRF, joint stress proxies, angles, torques, and energies. The project turns the simulator into a repeatable validation environment for those signals.






Outcome
Instead of only watching the robot move, we collect and save plots for walk, squat, jump without twist, and jump with twist. Those plots are then used to validate trends against the Euler-Lagrange model.

2. Problem Formulation & Assumptions
The model combines a detailed URDF robot with grouped validation channels so that the simulation remains interpretable at the report level.


Formulation
• Generalized coordinates are taken from the biped joint coordinates, with one leg often represented by q = [q1 ... q6]^T for derivation.
• Dynamic behavior is analyzed through total ground reaction force, joint stress proxy, joint angle, joint torque, kinetic energy, potential energy, and total energy.
• Validation plots are saved after simulation closure so every action produces a stable analysis folder output.

Assumptions
• Euler-Lagrange mechanics are used for derivation; Newtonian joint-force balance is not the primary framework.
• Hip, Knee, and Ankle are grouped for validation plots, even though the full simulation has more detailed joint coordinates.
• Stress is represented by a PyBullet reaction-force magnitude proxy because material stress data are not available in the URDF.

Validation Observations
• Walking produces sustained periodic GRF and energy variation over the longest duration.
• Squat stays mostly symmetric and is useful for checking vertical energy exchange.
• Jump actions compress the time horizon and highlight large event-driven torque and GRF changes.


Limitations
• The project uses parameter extraction from URDF rather than experimental parameter estimation, so it is a model-based system description more than classical system identification.
• Stress is a reaction-force proxy, not a material stress computation.
• Grouped Hip, Knee, and Ankle channels trade detail for interpretability at report scale.


SECTION 10
11 / 13


10. Conclusions & Reflections
The project now behaves like a dynamic analysis platform: it simulates motion, extracts interpretable signals, and saves post-run evidence for reporting.






What Worked
Euler-Lagrange mechanics provide a consistent analytical frame for masses, energies, and generalized torques. The saved analysis folder closes the loop between simulation and reporting.






Current Limits
The simulator still relies on PyBullet for detailed contact dynamics, and the grouped validation plots intentionally simplify the full 12-joint behavior into Hip, Knee, and Ankle trends.






Next Steps
Future work could add experimental identification, symbolic derivation of full M-C-G terms, or controller comparisons under the same saved validation pipeline.
