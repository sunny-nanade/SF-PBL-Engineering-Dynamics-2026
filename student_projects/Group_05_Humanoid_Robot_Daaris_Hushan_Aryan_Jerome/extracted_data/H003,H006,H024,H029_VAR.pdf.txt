1. Introduction
This report outlines the validation of the biped simulation created in PyBullet. The purpose is to verify that the
simulated responses are consistent with Euler-Lagrange rigid-body dynamics, expected energy behaviour, and
contact-based locomotion physics. The validation process uses three components: comparison with standard
analytical equations, energy verification, and action-based response analysis using walking, squatting, jumping
without torso twist, and jumping with torso twist.

5. Sensitivity Analysis
Sensitivity analysis for the current project is interpreted through task variation and controller/terrain parameters
rather than through fluid parameters as in the reference ROV report. The biped exposes stride length, step
height, and plane inclination during walking, while the action selection itself changes the required contact,
torque, and energy demand.
• Walking introduces periodic contact transfer and cyclic energy exchange.
• Squatting increases grouped knee and hip excursions while keeping the feet largely planted.
• Jumping increases peak ground reaction force and transient torque demand.
• Torso twist during jumping adds additional coupled rotational demand on the lower-body chain.
6. Discussion
The validation results support the consistency of the biped simulation with Euler-Lagrange mechanics. The
saved analysis confirms that the model produces finite and structured responses in ground reaction force,
grouped torque, grouped angle, and grouped energy. The contact-driven nature of walking and jumping is
visible through high GRF peaks, while the energy channels remain interpretable in terms of rigid-body motion
and gravitational storage.
There are still modelling limitations. The current report uses a PyBullet rigid-body engine rather than a fully
symbolic closed-form derivation of M(q), C(q, qdot), and G(q). Contact is also numerical and depends on
simulation settings rather than a dedicated analytical contact model. Even so, the system is appropriate for
project-level dynamic systems analysis and validation.
7. Conclusion
The validation of the biped simulation was performed by comparing it with Euler-Lagrange dynamic structure,
verifying energy behaviour, and reviewing action-dependent response trends from the saved simulation analysis
outputs. The resulting model behaves in a physically coherent manner and can be used for further educational
and project-level studies in humanoid lower-body dynamics.
