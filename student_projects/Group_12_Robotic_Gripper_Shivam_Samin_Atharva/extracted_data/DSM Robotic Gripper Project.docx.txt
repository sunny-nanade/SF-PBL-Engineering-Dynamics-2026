Phase 1: System Identification

1.1 Real-World Application
A robotic gripper is an end-effector attached to a robot arm that physically interacts with objects in the environment — grasping, manipulating, and releasing them with controlled force. Grippers are the hands of industrial robots, collaborative robots (cobots), and humanoid arms.
Real-world applications include:
Automotive assembly lines: picking and placing engine components with precision.
Pharmaceutical packaging: handling fragile vials and syringes without damage.
Warehouse logistics (e.g., Amazon Robotics): high-speed sorting and bin-picking.
Surgical robotics (e.g., da Vinci system): micro-scale, high-force-resolution grasping.
Food processing: soft-gripper variants handling deformable objects like bread or fruit.
The gripper mechanism selected for this project is a two-finger, single-actuator, parallel-jaw gripper driven by a DC motor through a lead-screw mechanism. This is Category A (Locomotion & Manipulation Dynamics) — it involves multi-DOF kinematics, contact force modelling, and actuator dynamics.

1.2 Inputs, Outputs, State Variables & Parameters

1.3 Degrees of Freedom (DOF)
The gripper system is modelled as a coupled electromechanical system with the following DOFs:
DOF 1 — Translational (jaw closure): jaw displacement x(t) along the closing axis.
DOF 2 — Rotational (motor shaft): angular position theta_m(t) of the DC motor.
DOF 3 — Electrical: armature current i(t) in the motor circuit.
Total: 3 generalised coordinates — {x, theta_m, i}. The system has 3 equations of motion (one mechanical translational, one mechanical rotational, one electrical).

1.4 Key Assumptions

1.5 Coordinate System & Sign Conventions
Origin: centre of the gripper (symmetry plane between jaws).
Positive x direction: jaw moving AWAY from centre (opening). Negative x: jaw closing.
Positive theta_m: motor shaft rotating in the direction that closes the jaw.
Positive current i: current flowing into the armature terminal (motoring mode).
Forces: tensile forces positive; compressive (gripping) forces reported as positive magnitudes.

Phase 2: Mathematical Modelling

2.1 Modelling Methodology — Why Newton-Euler?
For this gripper system, the Newton-Euler method is chosen over the Lagrangian approach for the following reasons:
The system has mixed translational (jaw) and rotational (motor) dynamics coupled through an explicit kinematic constraint (lead-screw). Newton-Euler lets us handle each subsystem separately and then apply the coupling constraint.
The electrical subsystem (motor circuit) is naturally handled by Kirchhoff's Voltage Law, which integrates seamlessly with the Newton-Euler mechanical equations.
The system has dissipative forces (damping, back-EMF) that appear naturally as force/torque terms in Newton-Euler formulation, whereas in Lagrange, care must be taken with Rayleigh dissipation functions.
The Lagrangian method would require computing kinetic and potential energies for all DOFs, which is more powerful for constrained multi-DOF systems but adds unnecessary complexity here.

2.2 Free Body Diagram — Description
The system is decomposed into three coupled subsystems. The FBD should be drawn on paper as per the guidelines. The key interactions are described below:
Subsystem A — Jaw (Translational)
Applied force from lead-screw: F_ls = (K_t * i * eta) / p  [N]  (force transmitted from motor through screw)
Spring restoring force: F_spring = -k_s * x  [N]  (return spring opposes jaw closing)
Viscous damping force: F_damp = -b * x_dot  [N]  (resists motion)
Contact/gripping force: F_grip (reaction from object when jaw contacts it; modelled as external load)
Inertia: m_jaw * x_ddot
Subsystem B — Motor Shaft (Rotational)
Electromagnetic torque: T_em = K_t * i  [N.m]
Load torque reflected from lead-screw: T_load = F_ls_reaction / p  [N.m]
Motor inertia: J_m * theta_m_ddot
Motor friction (lumped into b_m): b_m * omega_m
Subsystem C — Electrical Circuit (Motor Armature)
Supply voltage: V(t)  [V]
Resistive drop: R_m * i  [V]
Inductive drop: L_m * di/dt  [V]
Back-EMF: e_b = K_e * omega_m  [V]  (opposes applied voltage)
KVL: V(t) = R_m*i + L_m*(di/dt) + K_e*omega_m
Lead-Screw Kinematic Constraint
The lead-screw couples rotational and translational motion:
    x_dot = p * omega_m     (linear velocity = pitch * angular velocity)
    x_ddot = p * alpha_m    (linear acceleration = pitch * angular acceleration)

2.3 Kinematic Analysis
Let:
x = jaw displacement from fully-open position [m]
theta_m = motor shaft angle [rad]
p = lead-screw pitch [m/rad] — linear advance per radian of motor rotation
Kinematic constraint (geometric coupling):
    x = p * theta_m                                               ... (K1)
    x_dot = dx/dt = p * d(theta_m)/dt = p * omega_m              ... (K2)
    x_ddot = d^2x/dt^2 = p * d(omega_m)/dt = p * alpha_m        ... (K3)
This constraint reduces the independent DOFs from 2 (x and theta_m) to 1 mechanical DOF, since both are related. However, we retain both in the state vector for implementation clarity.

2.4 Force and Torque Analysis (Newton-Euler)
2.4.1 Jaw — Newton's Second Law (Translational)
Summing forces on the jaw mass m_jaw along the closing axis (positive = closing = negative x direction):
    m_jaw * x_ddot = F_ls - k_s * x - b * x_dot - F_grip        ... (E1)
where:
F_ls = force delivered by lead-screw to jaw = (T_em * eta) / p = (K_t * i * eta) / p
k_s * x = spring restoring force (spring pre-loaded when jaw is open)
b * x_dot = viscous damping at jaw slides
F_grip = gripping force (zero before contact; becomes constraint force at contact)
Substituting F_ls:
    m_jaw * x_ddot = (K_t * eta / p) * i - k_s * x - b * x_dot - F_grip  ... (E1')

2.4.2 Motor Shaft — Newton's Second Law (Rotational)
Summing torques on the motor shaft:
    J_m * alpha_m = T_em - T_load - b_m * omega_m               ... (E2)
where:
T_em = K_t * i  [N.m]  (electromagnetic torque)
T_load = (F_ls_jaw_reaction) * p / eta = (k_s*x + b*x_dot + F_grip) * p / eta  (reflected load)
b_m * omega_m = motor shaft viscous friction torque
Using kinematic constraint alpha_m = x_ddot / p:
    (J_m / p) * x_ddot = K_t * i - (k_s*x + b*x_dot + F_grip)*p/eta - b_m*(x_dot/p)  ... (E2')

2.4.3 Electrical Circuit — Kirchhoff's Voltage Law
Applying KVL around the motor armature loop:
    V(t) = R_m * i + L_m * (di/dt) + K_e * omega_m             ... (E3)
Substituting omega_m = x_dot / p:
    L_m * (di/dt) = V(t) - R_m * i - K_e * (x_dot / p)        ... (E3')

2.5 Equations of Motion (EOM) — Final Form
The three governing equations are (before contact, F_grip = 0):
Note: E1 and E2 can be combined (since x and theta_m are constrained) to yield a single effective mechanical equation with combined inertia M_eff = m_jaw + J_m/p^2.
Combined effective mechanical equation:
    M_eff * x''  +  B_eff * x'  +  K_eff * x  =  (K_t / p) * i   ... (FINAL MECH)
where:
M_eff = m_jaw + J_m / p^2       [kg]         (effective inertia)
B_eff = b + b_m / p^2            [N.s/m]      (effective damping)
K_eff = k_s                       [N/m]        (effective stiffness)
Forcing term = (K_t / p) * i     [N]          (electromagnetic force via lead-screw)

2.6 State-Space Representation
Define state vector: X_state = [x1, x2, x3] = [x, x_dot, i]
Then:
    x1' = x2                                                         ... (SS1)
    x2' = -(K_eff/M_eff)*x1  -  (B_eff/M_eff)*x2  +  (K_t/(p*M_eff))*x3   ... (SS2)
    x3' = -(K_e/(p*L_m))*x2  -  (R_m/L_m)*x3  +  (1/L_m)*V(t)   ... (SS3)

In matrix form:  X_state' = A * X_state + B * u

Output equation:  y = C * X_state   where C = [1, 0, 0]  (output is jaw position x)

import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from matplotlib.gridspec import GridSpec

# ── Parameters ─────────────────────────────────────────────
m_jaw = 0.15       # kg  - jaw mass
J_m   = 1e-5       # kg.m^2 - motor inertia
p     = 0.005      # m/rad  - lead-screw pitch
k_s   = 200.0      # N/m  - spring stiffness
b     = 2.0        # N.s/m - jaw damping
b_m   = 1e-4       # N.m.s/rad - motor damping
R_m   = 2.5        # Ohm
L_m   = 0.012      # H
K_t   = 0.08       # N.m/A
K_e   = 0.08       # V.s/rad
eta   = 0.85       # lead-screw efficiency
V_in  = 12.0       # Volts (step input)

# ── Effective parameters ───────────────────────────────────
M_eff = m_jaw + J_m / p**2   # effective inertia [kg]
B_eff = b + b_m / p**2       # effective damping [N.s/m]
K_eff = k_s                  # effective stiffness [N/m]

# ── State-space ODE ────────────────────────────────────────
# State: [x, x_dot, i]
def gripper_ode(t, state):
    x, x_dot, i_a = state
    # E1 (Jaw translational)
    x_ddot = (-(K_eff/M_eff)*x
              - (B_eff/M_eff)*x_dot
              + (K_t/(p*M_eff))*i_a)
    # E3 (Electrical)
    i_dot  = ((V_in - R_m*i_a - (K_e/p)*x_dot) / L_m)
    return [x_dot, x_ddot, i_dot]

# ── Solve ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
t_span = (0, 2.0)            # 0 to 2 seconds
t_eval = np.linspace(0, 2.0, 2000)
X0     = [0.05, 0.0, 0.0]   # IC: jaw 5 cm open, rest

sol = solve_ivp(gripper_ode, t_span, X0,
               method='RK45', t_eval=t_eval,
               rtol=1e-6, atol=1e-8)

t = sol.t
x     = sol.y[0]    # jaw displacement [m]
x_dot = sol.y[1]    # jaw velocity [m/s]
i_a   = sol.y[2]    # armature current [A]

# ── Energy calculations ────────────────────────────────────
KE = 0.5 * M_eff * x_dot**2                 # kinetic energy
PE = 0.5 * K_eff * x**2                     # spring potential
ME = KE + PE                                 # mechanical energy
P_input = V_in * i_a                        # electrical input power
P_diss  = R_m * i_a**2                      # resistive dissipation

# ── Plots ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
fig = plt.figure(figsize=(14, 10))
gs  = GridSpec(3, 2, figure=fig)

# Plot 1: Jaw displacement
ax1 = fig.add_subplot(gs[0, 0])
ax1.plot(t, x*1000, 'b-', linewidth=2)
ax1.set_xlabel('Time [s]'); ax1.set_ylabel('Jaw Displacement [mm]')
ax1.set_title('Jaw Position vs Time'); ax1.grid(True)

# Plot 2: Jaw velocity
ax2 = fig.add_subplot(gs[0, 1])
ax2.plot(t, x_dot*1000, 'g-', linewidth=2)
ax2.set_xlabel('Time [s]'); ax2.set_ylabel('Velocity [mm/s]')
ax2.set_title('Jaw Velocity vs Time'); ax2.grid(True)

# Plot 3: Armature current
ax3 = fig.add_subplot(gs[1, 0])
ax3.plot(t, i_a, 'r-', linewidth=2)
ax3.set_xlabel('Time [s]'); ax3.set_ylabel('Current [A]')
ax3.set_title('Armature Current vs Time'); ax3.grid(True)

# Plot 4: Energy
ax4 = fig.add_subplot(gs[1, 1])
ax4.plot(t, KE, label='KE [J]', linewidth=2)
ax4.plot(t, PE, label='PE [J]', linewidth=2)
ax4.plot(t, ME, label='ME=KE+PE [J]', linewidth=2, ls='--')
ax4.set_xlabel('Time [s]'); ax4.set_ylabel('Energy [J]')
ax4.set_title('Energy vs Time'); ax4.legend(); ax4.grid(True)

# Plot 5: Power
ax5 = fig.add_subplot(gs[2, :])
ax5.plot(t, P_input, label='Input Power [W]', linewidth=2)
ax5.plot(t, P_diss, label='Resistive Loss [W]', linewidth=2, ls='--')
ax5.set_xlabel('Time [s]'); ax5.set_ylabel('Power [W]')
ax5.set_title('Power Analysis'); ax5.legend(); ax5.grid(True)

plt.suptitle('Robotic Gripper Simulation — DSM Mini-Project',
             fontsize=13, fontweight='bold')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.savefig('gripper_simulation.png', dpi=150, bbox_inches='tight')
plt.show()

# ── Print steady state ─────────────────────────────────────
print(f'Steady-state jaw pos  : {x[-1]*1000:.2f} mm')
print(f'Steady-state current   : {i_a[-1]:.3f} A')
print(f'Gripping force (est)   : {K_t*i_a[-1]*eta/p:.2f} N')

3.4 Energy Analysis
As required by D3 and D4 assessment criteria, the energy analysis must be included:
Kinetic Energy
    KE(t) = (1/2) * M_eff * x_dot(t)^2                            [J]
Spring Potential Energy
    PE(t) = (1/2) * K_eff * x(t)^2                                [J]
Energy Dissipated by Damping
    E_diss(t) = integral of (B_eff * x_dot^2) dt   (computed numerically)
Electrical Energy Input
    E_in(t) = integral of (V_in * i(t)) dt   (computed numerically)
Energy Balance Check
At steady state (x_dot = 0, i = i_ss, x = x_ss):
KE = 0 (no motion)
PE = (1/2) * k_s * x_ss^2 (spring stores energy)
All electrical input has gone into: spring PE + resistive losses + mechanical work against damping
