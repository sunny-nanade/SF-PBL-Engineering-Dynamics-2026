INTRODUCTION 
Road safety is a critical aspect of modern transportation, and understanding the factors that lead to vehicle skidding is essential in preventing accidents. Skidding occurs when the frictional force between a vehicle’s tires and the road surface is insufficient to provide the necessary centripetal force for safe motion, especially while turning at higher speeds. This project focuses on determining the speed at which a model car begins to skid while moving along a curved path. By analysing the relationship between speed, radius of the curve, and frictional force, the experiment demonstrates important principles of physics such as circular motion and friction. The study aims to simulate real-life driving conditions using a controlled setup, allowing us to observe how increasing speed affects stability. The results of this project can help in understanding safe speed limits on curved roads and highlight the importance of tire grip and road conditions in preventing accidents.







Aim

To determine the maximum speed at which a car can move along a curved path without skidding.

Objectives
To understand the concept of centripetal force in circular motion
To study the role of friction between tires and road surface
To determine how speed affects stability of a moving vehicle
To calculate the safe speed for a given curve radius
To compare theoretical and experimental values

CONCLUSION (EXPANDED)*

From this experiment, it can be concluded that the stability of a vehicle on a curved path depends mainly on its speed, the radius of the curve, and the friction between the tires and the surface. As speed increases, the required centripetal force also increases. When this force exceeds the maximum frictional force available, the vehicle begins to skid.

The results confirm that higher friction allows higher safe speeds, while lower friction reduces the safe limit. This explains why vehicles are more likely to skid on wet or icy roads.

This project highlights the importance of controlling speed while driving on curves and demonstrates the practical application of physics concepts in real-life situations.
