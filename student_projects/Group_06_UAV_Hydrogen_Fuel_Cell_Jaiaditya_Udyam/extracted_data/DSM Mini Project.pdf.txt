System Overview
System Introduction & Motivation
● What is the system?
A quadrotor UAV powered by a hybrid H fuel cell + Li-ion battery bus. Four brushed-DC-motor-rotor pairs
2
generate thrust; a cascaded flight controller stabilises it.
○
●
Why does it matter?
H UAVs promise -3x endurance over Li-ion drones but fuel cells respond slowly (seconds). Batteries are fast
2
but heavy. A hybrid bus gets both: endurance from H agility from the battery.
2
●
●
PBL Question : Can we model 4 coupled physics domains – electrochemical, electrical, electromechanical,
rigid-body-6-DoF – in one simulation, and show that the battery absorbs the fast transients while the fuel
cell supplies the steady hover power?

Key Insights
Conclusions & Reflections
●
Key Finding: the hybrid architecture is validated. Simulation confirms "slow FC + fast battery"
division of labour: battery supplies ~80 A transient during takeoff, fuel cell ramps over ~3 s to carry
the steady 7 A hover load. 90% of input energy leaves as aerodynamic work — hovering IS
air-moving.
●
What a textbook couldn't teach us: the signs in the mixer matrix are where all real projects fail. A
τ
single sign error in the rotor-direction vector drives yaw into divergence. We had to re-derive =
z
C ( ω 2− ω 2+ ω 2− ω 2) three times before the yaw channel was stable. Textbook problems don't have this
q 1 2 3 4
coupling-sign trap.
●
What we would do differently: derive the mixer and fix the rotor-direction convention before writing
any code. We wrote the simulator first and spent half our debugging time chasing sign errors.
●
Course units most critical: FBD analysis (CO2), Newton-Euler for rigid bodies (CO4), and Energy
methods for validation (CO5).
