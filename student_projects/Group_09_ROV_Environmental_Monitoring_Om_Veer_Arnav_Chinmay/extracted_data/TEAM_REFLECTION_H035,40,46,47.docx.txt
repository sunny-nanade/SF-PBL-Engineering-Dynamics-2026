Q1: What was the single hardest technical challenge you faced in deriving the mathematical model? How did you resolve it?
One of the most challenging issues we encountered involved optimizing the codes on our individual desktops in such a way that they would run effectively. We had to make internal changes to the codes so they would correspond with the hardware specifications of our systems. Additionally, debugging using AI takes considerably longer than it would normally take because certain versions of files must be installed to be able to utilize the software.

Q2: Which Course Outcome (CO1-CO5) was most challenging for your team? Why?
Although you are knowledgeable about the equations, testing them in the ocean on a six-degree-of-freedom body would require several trials because of the additional forces such as thrust, drag, buoyancy, and restoring forces acting on the system when it was subjected to various conditions. Hence CO3 was the most challenging course outcome for our team. 
Q3: What would you do differently if you started the project over?
In our modelling process we start with a model that has one degree of freedom. We validate it before moving on to build a model with six degrees of freedom. This approach makes it easier to debug issues.

We also make our assumptions clear, at the beginning of the modelling process.
Q4: What did you learn from this project that a regular textbook problem could NOT teach you?
This study showed that systems in life are not perfect. They have things like drag force that does not work in a line, energy that gets lost and tiny mistakes in the way we model them can make a big difference, in what happens. Unlike the problems you find in textbooks we had to check our model by trying it out with real things.
