What is the system? 

The system is a multi-terrain mobile robotic platform that combines wheels and leg-like suspension (quadrupedal mechanism) to move efficiently across uneven, rough, and complex terrains.

More specifically, it is a hybrid quadrupedal-wheeled robot with:
Active suspension using servomotors 
Four-bar linkage mechanism for each leg 
Skid-steering drive system 
Independent wheel actuation 

Why does it matter? (Engineering application)

This system is important because traditional wheeled robots struggle on uneven or off-road environments, while purely legged robots are complex and expensive.
This hybrid approach solves that gap.
Key engineering applications:
Search and rescue missions (disaster zones, rubble) 
Military and surveillance robots 
Planetary exploration (e.g., Mars rovers) 
Agricultural field robots 
Remote sensing in rough terrains


1. System Introduction & Motivation
This design allows the robot to:
Adjust its ground clearance dynamically 
Maintain stability on slopes and obstacles 
Move using wheels while adapting like a legged system 

What question are we answering? (PBL problem statement)

Problem Statement:
How can a multi-terrain robotic system be developed using a hybrid quadrupedal-wheeled mechanism with active suspension to ensure dynamic stability, adaptive ground clearance, and efficient locomotion across rough and unstructured environments?

Answer:
First, the robot uses a quadrupedal structure combined with wheels, where each leg is equipped with a wheel. This allows the system to:
Move efficiently on flat surfaces like a wheeled robot 
Adapt to uneven terrain like a legged robot 

Second, an active suspension system using servomotors is implemented. These servomotors control the position of each leg through a four-bar linkage mechanism, enabling:
Adjustment of ground clearance based on terrain conditions 
Maintenance of balance on slopes and irregular surfaces 
Reduction of mechanical stress using shock absorbers 

Third, dynamic stability is ensured through mathematical modeling, particularly:
Helps maintain the robot’s center of gravity within safe limits 
Control of pitch and roll motion to prevent overturning 

Additionally, a skid-steering mechanism is used for locomotion. This allows the robot to:
Change direction without complex steering linkages 
Improve maneuverability in confined or rough environments

2. Problem Formulation & Assumptions
DOF:
1. Planar Motion DOF (Body) = 2
        -Forward/lateral motion 
        -Yaw (rotation about vertical axis) 
 2. Suspension DOF = 4
        -4 independent servo-controlled joints 
        -Each leg (wheel assembly) can move vertically via active suspension 
      Each servo = 1 DOF → 4 DOF
 Total DOF = 2 (motion) + 4 (suspension) = 6 DOF

Key assumptions:
Rigid body – deformation negligible
Planar motion – out-of-plane effects ignored
Continuous wheel-ground contact
Small-angle approximation
Symmetric geometry (at 180° configuration)
Viscous damping proportional to velocity
