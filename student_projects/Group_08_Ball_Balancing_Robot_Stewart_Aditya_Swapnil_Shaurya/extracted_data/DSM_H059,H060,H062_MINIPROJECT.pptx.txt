1. System Introduction & Motivation
What is the system?A Stewart platform simulation that tilts using servo control to balance a ball via a PID feedback loop.

Why does it matter?
Demonstrates key concepts in control systems, robotics, and real-time stabilization used in practical engineering applications.

Problem Statement:Develop a control system that maintains the ball at the center by dynamically correcting its position under external disturbances.
2. Problem Formulation & Assumptions
Inputs:u = [α (tilt about x-axis), β (tilt about y-axis)](Controlled via PID → converted to servo angles)
Outputs:Ball position (x, y, z)Ball velocity (vx, vy, vz)Platform orientation (roll ≈ β, pitch ≈ α)Error (distance from center)Control response (tilt angles)
State Variables:X = [x, y, vx, vy, α, β, ∫e, de/dt]
Parameters:Ball mass ≈ 0.0027 kgGravity = 9.81 m/s²Platform height (H₀)Friction coefficientRestitution factorPID gains (Kp, Ki, Kd)
Forces:Gravitational forceNormal reaction from platformFrictional forceExternal disturbance forces (user input)
DOF:DOF = 2 (ball motion on surface) + 2 (platform tilt)Translational: x, yRotational: α (pitch), β (roll)
Key Assumptions (JUSTIFIED):(1) Platform behaves as a rigid body(2) Small-angle tilt approximation(3) Rolling with limited slip (friction modeled)(4) Ideal actuator response (instant tilt change)(5) Uniform gravity, no air resistance
