System Identification Report
The selected system is a Stewart Platform Ball Balancing System, which is a closed-loop control system used to stabilize a ball on a tilting platform. The platform orientation is adjusted dynamically using a control algorithm to maintain the ball at a desired position, typically the centre.
Real-World Application
This system represents practical applications in robotics, motion control, and stabilization systems, such as self-balancing robots, precision positioning platforms, and flight simulators. It demonstrates fundamental concepts of feedback control and dynamic system response.
System Definition
Inputs
u = [α, β]
where α and β are the tilt angles of the platform along two perpendicular axes, controlled using a PID controller.
Outputs
Ball position (x, y, z) 
Ball velocity (vx, vy, vz) 
Platform orientation (roll, pitch) 
Error (distance from desired position) 
State Variables
X = [x, y, vx, vy, α, β, ∫e, de/dt]
where x and y represent ball position, vx and vy represent velocities, and the remaining terms correspond to control states.
Key Parameters
Ball mass ≈ 0.0027 kg 
Gravitational acceleration = 9.81 m/s² 
Friction coefficient 
Platform height 
PID gains (Kp, Ki, Kd) 
Degrees of Freedom (DOF)
DOF = 4
Translational: Ball motion in x and y directions 
Rotational: Platform tilt about two axes 
Justification:
The ball moves freely on a 2D surface, while the platform tilts along two axes to control its motion. This combination results in four effective degrees of freedom.
Key Assumptions
The platform behaves as a rigid body 
Small-angle approximation is valid for tilt angles 
The ball rolls with limited slipping (friction modeled) 
Actuators are ideal with no delay 
Air resistance is negligible 
The system operates in a controlled environment
