1. System Introduction & Motivation
What is the system? (Real-world description)
This is a mobile manipulator — a ground robot that can both navigate an environment and physically interact with objects. Specifically, it combines:
A Clearpath Husky — a 38 kg, four-wheel differential-drive unmanned ground vehicle (UGV)
A KUKA iiwa — a 7-degree-of-freedom robotic arm mounted rigidly on top of the Husky

Why does it matter? (Engineering application)
Mobile manipulators are one of the central problems in modern robotics. Unlike a fixed arm (limited to a small workspace) or a wheeled robot (can move but can't grab things), a mobile manipulator can traverse an environment and physically act on it. This matters enormously in:
Planetary exploration — rovers like NASA's Perseverance must navigate terrain and collect rock samples autonomously
Warehouse logistics — robots that autonomously fetch, carry, and place goods (Amazon, Boston Dynamics Stretch)
Search and rescue — robots deployed into hazardous areas to retrieve objects or operate valves
Field agriculture — harvesting robots that navigate rows and pick produce
Construction and inspection — manipulators on mobile platforms inspecting or repairing structures
The specific challenge demonstrated here — navigate, pick, transport, place — is the canonical "pick-and-place" task and serves as a foundation for nearly all applied mobile manipulation research.


1. System Introduction & Motivation
What question are we answering? (PBL problem statement)
Can a robot autonomously navigate an obstacle-filled environment, use its arm to retrieve a target object, and deliver it to a goal — with no human input?
This is the classic autonomous mobile pick-and-place problem, and the code answers it by implementing: ray-based obstacle avoidance, waypoint navigation, stuck recovery, inverse kinematics arm control, and constraint-based grasping.

Key Assumptions (JUSTIFIED):
(1) Rigid body throughout — both the Husky chassis and KUKA arm links are treated as rigid; deformation is negligible at the low speeds and forces involved (max 950 N wheel force, <2 m/s travel speed).
(2) Planar base motion — the Husky operates on a flat ground plane; z-position is constant, out-of-plane forces are zeroed by the plane contact constraint. Only x, y, and yaw are controlled.
(3) Viscous damping on all joints — linearDamping and angularDamping are explicitly set on both the base and arm links, modeling drag proportional to velocity. This stabilizes the simulation and approximates real actuator back-EMF and friction behavior.
(4) Grasp modeled as a fixed constraint — true contact/friction grasping is replaced by a JOINT_FIXED PyBullet constraint between the end-effector and sample block, with maxForce = 50 N. This assumes a successful grasp once the end-effector reaches within threshold distance.
(5) Perfect ray sensing — the 17-beam ray scanner returns exact hit distances with no noise, modeling an ideal lidar. Real sensors would introduce measurement uncertainty.

4. Mathematical Derivation (Part 1)
Method Selection
Method: Lagrange Mechanics.
Why this method? The system consists of a mobile base and a 7-DOF arm rigidly coupled. Using the Lagrangian method avoids the need to explicitly calculate the internal constraint forces at the mounting point (z = 0.25) that a Newtonian approach would require.
